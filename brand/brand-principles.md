# Brand Principles

Five ideas that every decision in this system answers to. They come from what the Foundation does and from what its existing identity already says.

## 1. Hope is practical

The brand promises restored hope and delivers it as a place: a lodge, a pond, a pool, a music hall, a week a family gets to keep. Design should feel the same: warm, but concrete. Real photographs of the real place. A donation button that says exactly what it builds. No abstractions, no metaphors for caring; the hands in the mark are already doing that work.

## 2. One organization, clearly in charge

Cotton Foundation owns and operates Taylor's Place and hosts CrawFest to fund it. Every page, sign, shirt, and post makes that visible: the lockup at the top, the endorsement under the Taylor's Place mark, "A Cotton Foundation event" where CrawFest stands alone. A donor should never wonder whether Taylor's Place is a different charity.

## 3. The place is the flagship

Taylor's Place is the most important thing the Foundation has, and the identity gives it the most room: the gold call to action on the home page, its own mark, its own tagline, the warmest register in the palette (gold on navy), the best photography.

## 4. Established, not institutional

The Foundation should look like it has been here a while and will be here for the families who need it: a serif with roots, navy, a mark that could be cast in bronze. It is not a hospital, not a corporation, and not a charity template. No slab type, no chrome, no teal-and-orange, no "solutions." It speaks like a neighbor with resources, not like a vendor.

## 5. Independent, and respectful of the people in the picture

Cotton Foundation stands on its own: no other organization's name, history, or mark shares its space. And the families at Taylor's Place are living through the hardest thing that will ever happen to them. The design never uses them for effect: no illness photography, no staged gratitude, no before-and-after. Faces appear with consent and with dignity, and the brand's restraint (few colors, quiet type, room to breathe) is a form of that respect.

## The test

> Would a family arriving at the Taylor's Place gate, a volunteer setting up CrawFest, and a donor reading the annual letter all recognize the same organization, and would each of them feel respected by how it looks?
