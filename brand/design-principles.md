# Design Principles

How the brand principles become visual decisions. These are the rules a designer (or Claude Design) applies when the guidelines do not cover a case.

## 1. Navy holds, gold lights, white breathes

Navy is structure: headings, buttons, the impact band, the footer, the overlay on every photograph. Gold is attention: one call to action, one eyebrow, the boll in the mark, the statistics on navy. White is most of the page. When a layout feels heavy, the answer is more white; when it feels flat, the answer is one gold element, not two.

## 2. Serif for feeling, sans for doing

Cormorant Garamond says who we are and why; PT Sans says what to do and where. A heading is always the serif; a button, label, or form is always the sans. Never reverse them.

## 3. Photographs are the warmth

The palette and type are restrained on purpose so that photographs of the land, the lodge, and the families carry the emotion. A page without a real photograph should be rare; a page with a stock photograph should not exist. The navy overlay keeps every photograph in the family.

## 4. One idea per section

Eyebrow, heading, two sentences, one action. The old site already worked this way; keep it. If a section needs two headings, it is two sections. If it needs two calls to action, one of them is a text link.

## 5. Taylor's Place gets the gold button

Wherever the Foundation and Taylor's Place are offered side by side, the flagship takes the primary (Hope Gold) action and the general or secondary ask (Support Us, Contact) takes the outline button. The hierarchy of the organization is also the hierarchy of the page.

## 6. The mark is whole, and it stands alone

The boll and the hands are one image. No partial marks, no icon extraction, no hands without the boll, no boll as a bullet. The mark is used alone, in the lockup, or as an endorsement beneath Taylor's Place, and that is all. It never shares a lockup with another organization's logo.

## 7. Build the system, not new logos

Campaigns are descriptors. Events are editions. Only a property gets a mark, and Taylor's Place already has one. When something new needs an identity, set its name in the Display Serif under the Foundation lockup and stop.

## 8. Rounded but not soft

24 px corners on cards, panels, and photographs; 8 px on buttons; nothing sharper, nothing rounder. The corners are the brand's gentleness. Pills, blobs, and circles (other than the social avatar) tip it into generic-charity territory.

## 9. Accessible by default

Body text in Ink, gold text only where it passes, buttons with dark text on gold, eyebrows that pass on their ground, 16 px minimum body, focus states visible in navy. The measured contrast table in `guidelines/colors.md` is the reference. A design that fails it is not finished.

## 10. Say less, mean it

Fewer words, fewer colors, fewer elements. If a piece is doing something the guidelines do not require, remove it and see whether it was needed. The Foundation's work is serious; its design earns trust by not shouting.
