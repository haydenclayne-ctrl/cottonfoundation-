# Voice and Tone

How Cotton Foundation writes, drawn from the Taylor's Place copy on the old website. That copy's voice is consistent and worth keeping; this document names it so the new site, emails, and social posts match it. Copy from the old site about anything other than the Foundation and Taylor's Place is not a reference.

## The voice in three words

**Warm. Direct. Grounded.**

- **Warm:** it talks about families, memories, comfort, hope, and it means it. "A safe haven where families can heal, rebuild, and thrive."
- **Direct:** short declaratives, active verbs, first person plural. "Help us build a community where families come together to find healing."
- **Grounded:** it says what exists, with places and specifics. "Thirty acres west of Katy, Texas." "Phase 1 of Taylor's Place is the main lodge."

## How it sounds

- **We, not the Foundation.** "We'd love to hear you!", "Help us build a community…", "We aim to invite the first family…". Third person only in the legal line.
- **Verbs of restoration:** restore, rebuild, reconnect, create, heal, rest, strengthen, lift, welcome. Avoid "empower," "leverage," "impactful," "solutions."
- **Concrete nouns:** the lodge, the pond, the pool, the music hall, the playground, the porch, crawfish. Not "resources" when you can say what they are.
- **Hope as a noun with a verb attached:** "restoring hope," "find healing," "create lasting memories." Never hope alone as a slogan.
- **Sentences:** 12–22 words in body copy. Headlines 3–7 words. Eyebrows 2–5 words.
- **Numbers** are figures in statistics (30 acres, 2 phases, $760,000) and words in sentences ("thirty acres"). No figures are hard-coded into templates; every statistic is confirmed before use.

## Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Example (from the old site or in its voice) |
|---|---|---|
| Home and About | Confident, warm, institutional | "Cotton Foundation is dedicated to restoring hope for children and families facing life-altering illnesses." |
| Taylor's Place | Tender, unhurried, specific | "…where families with terminally ill members can find respite and create lasting memories." |
| The Tom Wallace story | Plain, respectful, no euphemism | "During his four-year battle with cancer, rather than lamenting his illness, Tom devoted his time to his friends, family, and to giving to others." |
| Construction and progress | Practical, forward-looking | "Taylor's Place officially broke ground in 2025, and we're thrilled to share our latest progress with you." |
| Donation and sponsorship asks | Direct, grateful, concrete about use | "Whether sponsoring a complete package or donating, there are plenty of ways to support Taylor's Place." |
| CrawFest | Festive, plain-spoken | "…an event filled with music, fellowship and, of course, thousands of pounds of crawfish." |
| Legal and tax lines | Neutral | "The Cotton Charitable Foundation is a registered 501(c)(3) organization. All donations are tax-deductible to the fullest extent of the law." |

## Words to use and to avoid

Use: restore, rebuild, hope, families, respite, memories, comfort, dignity, together, sanctuary, the lodge, the land, Taylor's Place.

Avoid: patients, victims, beneficiaries, clients, empower, impactful, synergy, solutions, leverage, world-class, journey (as a metaphor), "give back" (fine once in Tom's story, tired elsewhere), and any phrase that describes the Foundation by reference to another organization's work or history.

## Taglines

Working lines, in the ground and in the copy, unapproved as official taglines (decision log Q4):

- **Restoring Hope** — the Foundation.
- **Creating Memories** — Taylor's Place, cut into the gate sign.
- **Crawfish for a Cause** — the CrawFest eyebrow.
