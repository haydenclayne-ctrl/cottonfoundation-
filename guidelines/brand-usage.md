# Brand Usage: do and do not

The short rulebook. Every other guideline explains why; this one is the checklist to run before anything ships.

## Do

- Lead with the full-color lockup on white, or the white lockup on navy. Those two cover 95% of uses.
- Keep Cotton Foundation visibly in charge: the lockup on every page, the endorsement under every Taylor's Place mark, "A Cotton Foundation event" wherever CrawFest stands alone.
- Keep the Foundation's space its own: the lockup appears alone, never beside another organization's logo, never with a "since" date or a history that is not the Foundation's.
- Open every section with the eyebrow + heading + copy trio.
- Use one gold call to action per screen and make it the action that matters most (usually Taylor's Place or Donate).
- Put photographs under the navy overlay before putting type on them.
- Write "Restoring Hope" as the Foundation's idea and "Creating Memories" as Taylor's Place's; do not swap them.
- State the legal name and 501(c)(3) status in the tax-deductibility line on donation materials (after the owner confirms the legal name, Q2).
- Keep the type to Cormorant Garamond and PT Sans.
- Proof Pantone and thread against physical swatches before ordering anything permanent.
- Save explorations in `concepts/`, approved work in `assets/`, and record the move in `brand/decision-log.md`.

## Do not

- Do not redraw, retype, recolor, outline, shadow, or rotate the mark or wordmark.
- Do not use the full-color lockup on navy, gold, or photography.
- Do not put Hope Gold text on white, Harvest Gold text on navy, or Horizon Blue text on navy.
- Do not set body text in Slate; it is for captions.
- Do not add a color, a typeface, an icon style, a pattern, or a tagline without a decision-log entry.
- Do not create a new logo for a campaign or event. Campaigns are descriptors; Taylor's Place is the only property with its own mark.
- Do not reference, name, or co-brand with any other company or organization anywhere in Foundation materials; do not reuse old-site copy that does.
- Do not merge the Foundation mark with any partner or sponsor logo; sponsors are a logo row, not a co-brand.
- Do not use stock photography, illness imagery, black overlays, gradients, or filters.
- Do not write "Cotton Foundation" in a font next to the mark as a substitute for the lockup.
- Do not invent facts: founding year, dollar figures, family details, opening dates. Use only what the owner has confirmed and flag anything uncertain (decision log Q2, Q4, Q8).

## Sponsors and partners

Sponsor and partner logos appear in their own colors on white, in a row or grid, all at equal visual weight (normalize by area, not by width), under the eyebrow OUR SPONSORS or OUR PARTNERS. The Foundation lockup is not placed in the row; it is on the page header. On event banners, the sponsor block is a separate white panel beneath the Foundation's navy panel. A sponsor is thanked; it is never co-branded.

**Taylor's Place is not co-branding.** Taylor's Place is the Foundation's; the endorsement rule in `sub-brands.md` applies.

## The reject list

If a piece of work looks like any of these, it is wrong: a generic charity template (a heart logo, a hands-and-globe icon, teal and orange); a hospital or hospice brochure; a rustic ranch brand (rope borders, longhorns, distressed type) for Taylor's Place; a crawfish-boil poster for anything but CrawFest; a page with three golds fighting; a hero with black type on an unfiltered photo; a footer with two logos; a Canva logo.

## The test

> Would a family arriving at the Taylor's Place gate, a volunteer setting up CrawFest, and a donor reading the annual letter all recognize the same organization, and would each of them feel respected by how it looks?

If yes, ship it.
