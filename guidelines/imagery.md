# Imagery

Photography carries this brand's emotion. The palette is disciplined and the type is quiet so that the pictures of the land and the people can do the talking. Every photograph should answer one of two questions: *where does healing happen?* or *who is this for?*

## The subjects

**1. Taylor's Place, the land.** Thirty acres west of Katy: the lodge, the pond, the live oaks, the long drive, the gate with its sign, the pool at sunset, the porch. Wide, calm, early or late light. Aerials from the construction phase are useful; from opening onward, photograph the real place at ground level and retire the renderings.

**2. Taylor's Place, the people.** Families on the porch, a child with a fishing rod, a grandmother in the music hall, staff setting a table. Always with consent, never staged as gratitude, never in a hospital setting. Show the week they get to keep, not the reason they need it.

**3. The Foundation's people and events.** Tom Wallace's family photographs (as they are, unretouched), Taylor Odum, volunteers and sponsors at CrawFest, the crawfish boil itself. Treat these as portraits, not stock.

## Direction

- **Light:** natural, early morning or late afternoon on the land; open shade or overcast for people. No flash, no studio.
- **Color:** true to life, slightly warm, medium saturation. The navy and gold of the brand should appear naturally (the sky, dry grass, the sign), not be tinted in.
- **Composition:** eye level or slightly above; one clear subject; room for type when the image will sit under a headline (a quiet sky or ground on one side).
- **People:** faces are welcome and consent is required (a signed release on file for anyone recognizable, especially children and families at Taylor's Place). Show families with dignity: no posed handshakes, no illness photography, no before-and-after.
- **Crop:** 16:9 for heroes and cards, 3:2 for split sections, 1:1 for social, 4:5 for portrait posts.

## The navy overlay

When type sits on a photograph, the photograph sits under **Foundation Navy at about 72% opacity** (`--cf-overlay-hero`). This is the single most important imagery rule: it keeps every hero, CTA band, and video poster in the palette and makes white type readable. Do not use black overlays, gradients to transparent, or blur. If the photograph does not work under the navy overlay, it is not a hero photograph; use it in a card instead.

## Renderings and illustration

Architectural renderings of Taylor's Place (aerials, the entrance arch, the chapel, the music hall, the pool, the playground) are acceptable in the construction-progress context, labeled as renderings. They should not be used as hero images once photography of the built lodge exists. The brand has no illustration style; do not commission one without a decision-log entry. The only drawn elements are the mark and the Taylor's Place roofline.

## Video

The Taylor's Place update video ("What's Happening at Taylor's Place") sets the standard: a 16:9 embed with a rounded 24 px frame, a poster image from the video, the gold play button. Titles in video use Cormorant Garamond and PT Sans in the brand colors; lower thirds are navy panels with white type.

## Avoid

- Stock photography of any kind. If a real photograph does not exist, use a navy panel with type.
- Hospital, clinical, or illness imagery. The brand shows the respite, not the diagnosis.
- Heavy filters, HDR, vignettes, black-and-white treatments (the archival Tom Wallace photographs are the exception and stay as they are).
- Photographs with other organizations' logos in frame unless a sponsor is the subject of a sponsor post.
- Text-heavy graphics as posts; a photograph with one line beats a poster with six.
